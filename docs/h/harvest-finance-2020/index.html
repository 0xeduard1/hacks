Harvest Finance

🎭 Oracle manipulation
💥 $34Mabout $33.8M lost by the vaults, about $24M kept by the attacker, about $2.5M sent back Oct 26, 2020dateEthereumchain🟡 mediumto understand

The vaults priced their shares using a Curve pool that a flash loan could nudge. Buy shares cheap, push the price back, sell them dear, repeat.

  1. 🏰

    What was it?

    Harvest Finance was a yield farming protocol. Users deposited stablecoins like USDC and USDT into vaults, which put the money to work in Curve's Y pool.

  2. 💥

    What happened

    In about seven minutes, the attacker used flash loans to swing stablecoin prices inside the Curve pool, deposited into the vaults at a skewed share price and withdrew at a better one. Each round made a small profit; around 30 rounds added up to millions.

  3. 🎭

    The bug, in plain words

    The vault valued its shares from a live pool price that one large trade could move. Its built-in check only reacted to big price swings, so moves of about 1% passed silently.

  4. 🛡️

    How it could have been stopped

    Never value deposits and withdrawals from a price that can be moved inside one transaction. Use time-weighted prices, tighter checks, or block deposit and withdraw in the same transaction.

  5. 🧙

    The lesson for an auditor

    If one trade can move your price, a flash loan can move it a lot, for free, as many times as it likes.

Meet the monster: 🎭 The Price Faker

A protocol trusts a price that can be pushed around, usually a thinly traded token. The attacker pumps it, uses it as collateral, and borrows real assets.
